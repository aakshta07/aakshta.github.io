n8n leadership-reporting pipeline

Three chained n8n workflows that turn raw HR data into AI-written leadership reports

The problem

Leaders at different levels needed a monthly view of 1:1 coverage, engagement and retention risk. Building it by hand from spreadsheets was slow and gave different answers each time, and one report format could not suit both a founder and a team leader.

How it’s put together

  1. Workflow 1: hierarchyBuilds a leader-to-team map from employee and manager data
  2. Workflow 2: data prepCalculates 1:1 coverage, engagement participation, mood, sentiment spread, high-risk indicators and retention measures per leader
  3. Workflow 3: reportA webhook triggers a Vertex AI/Gemini step that writes the report
  4. Quality checkEach report returns structured output with a parse-success flag, so a failed report is caught before anyone reads it

Square boxes are fixed logic. The rounded dashed box is where a language model is involved.

Built with form and manual triggers, Google Sheets reads and writes, batching, loops, wait nodes, structured JSON parsing, error fallbacks and validation of source values.

The guardrails

All numbers are calculated before the model sees them, so it writes the report but never recalculates sentiment or mood. Prompts are written for each leadership level (founders, SVP/VP, SD), and senior-leader reports are barred from showing individual employee names. Every report follows the same structure: summary, highlights, lowlights, recommendations, coverage and retention.

What changed

  • Every leader gets the same metrics, calculated the same way
  • Became the base for HR-Agent-OS, below

What I learned

  • Keep the calculations fixed and use the model only to write.
  • Build the failure check in from the start, not after the first bad report.
  • Privacy rules belong in the design, not in a reviewer’s checklist.

HR-Agent-OS

A multi-agent Slack copilot for HR business partners (HRBPs) and leaders

The problem

HR leaders had no visibility into whether 1:1s were happening, and employees said they were infrequent. No data existed to say either way.

How it’s put together

  1. Two Slack appsOne assistant for HRBPs, one for private employee check-ins
  2. Two deterministic workersFixed rules, same answer every time
  3. Two LLM analysis agentsOne reads employee check-ins, one reads HRBP 1:1 notes
  4. Human-in-the-loop reviewA person checks before it’s acted on
  5. DashboardsLooker and an internal tool

Square boxes are fixed logic. The rounded dashed box is where a language model is involved.

Data sits in SQLite. Employee wikis are markdown files. Access is limited to HRBPs.

The sourcing logic

I wrote the logic that tells each HRBP who to meet this month, using survey and sentiment data. It sorts people into four buckets that never overlap, in priority order: high performers not met in three months, people not met in six months who show low or falling mood, anyone else not met in six months, and people reaching a tenure milestone. Each HRBP’s list is capped so it stays something they can act on.

The guardrails

The AI only interprets a conversation after the record exists. It can summarise what was said but cannot change it, and the analysing agents are told never to follow instructions found inside a transcript. The employee check-in agent only listens and never gives advice. A person reviews the monthly leader report before it goes out.

The mechanical work it removes

Booking a 1:1 from a one-line Slack message, pulling 1:1 notes in without manual logging, keeping one running notes file per employee, and drafting the month-end report for each leader.

What changed

  • Serves 26 HRBPs and 82 leaders, up to co-founders
  • Monthly HRBP report: from ~2-3 hours to under 10 minutes
  • ~50-75 hours a month saved across HRBPs
  • ~21 1:1s per HRBP per month now tracked

What I learned

  • Validate the problem with the people who live it, and get their buy-in, before writing anything.
  • Stress-test repeatedly, then pilot with a smaller group before wider use. A quality-check dashboard made output problems visible.
  • Keep fixed logic fixed and use the language model only where judgement or wording is needed.
  • Restricting access early made the tool easier to trust.
  • Treat anything a model reads, such as a transcript, as untrusted input.

Sales deck generator

A Slack agent that builds on-brand, merchant-specific decks

The problem

Every merchant-specific sales deck depended on the design team, so turnaround took days or weeks.

How it’s put together

  1. Request in SlackWhere sales already works
  2. AgentBuilds the deck for that merchant
  3. On-brand deckReturned in the same thread

What changed

  • ~200 users, ~25 decks a month
  • Turnaround from days or weeks to minutes
  • Sales no longer depends on design

What I learned

  • Putting the tool where people already work removes most of the adoption effort.
  • Removing a dependency matters as much as saving time.

Learning session automation

Scheduling, attendance and documentation for ~200 org-wide sessions a month

The problem

Running learning sessions across the organisation meant weeks of manual effort on scheduling, invites, attendance and documentation.

How it’s put together

  1. SchedulingEach session starts as one row in a sheet. The ID and duration are generated automatically
  2. Invites
  3. AttendanceTracked per session. Processed rows are marked done
  4. DocumentationProcessed attendance is copied to a central log

Built with Google Apps Script, Google APIs and n8n. Each step has its own on/off switch and debug logging, so I can turn one off or trace it without touching the rest.

What changed

  • Effort cut from weeks to under 30 minutes

What I learned

  • Automating the whole chain, not one step, is what turned weeks into minutes.
  • Build in switches and logging from the start, so a step can be turned off or traced without breaking the rest.

How it’s put together

Teaching teams to build their own

Workshops, a guardrails course, office hours, a hackathon and champions

The problem

Teams like Sales, People Ops, Customer Success and Finance had real workflows worth automating but no engineers to build them. Many had never built anything with AI, and any wider rollout had to be safe.

What I ran

  1. Hands-on sessionsn8n, Claude and Gemini for Sales, People Ops, CIO, Customer Success and teams in Malaysia, Singapore and the US
  2. Course with guardrailsBuilt inside the company’s internal AI tool, including a security module
  3. Hackathon and office hoursMentoring and sprint-based delivery for builders, including people with no engineering background
  4. ChampionsEvery team has an identified AI champion

What changed

  • 11 sessions for 1,200+ people in a single month
  • 1,700+ builders across 400+ teams in the hackathon, almost half without engineers
  • 12 demos now in talks for production
  • The course is still in use without me
  • Teams shipped their own tools, including a leaderboard for Sales and a payroll assistant for Finance

What I learned

  • Build live with a team on their own problem. It works better than demos.
  • Put security in the first session, so teams build safely from day one.
  • Champions in each team keep the knowledge there after I leave.

Currently building: a new-joiner learning bot

In planning. Nothing here has shipped yet.

The problem

New joiners need to work through a required learning path in order. The assessments sit in a learning platform with no API, so the bot can’t see completions in real time.

How it’s designed

  1. Joiner triggerHR data checked daily, matched on start date
  2. Slack journeyA gated path, with weekday nudges only for people with open actions
  3. Completion checkLearner declares, a nightly report confirms
  4. ReportingDeduped exceptions and a daily digest, no intervention in the journey

The learner never waits on batch data, and a manual pull covers a missed nightly report. The core logic doesn’t depend on Slack, so it can be dry-run on its own. Scope stays small until pilot data exists. A separate monthly check-in survey tool, kept isolated from this one, is the next build.
